Scientific Integrity & Candidate Verification Guidelines

True Motion Candidate

Shows paired positive and negative peaks (dipole) along the expected proper motion or parallax trajectory.

Variable Flux Candidate

Shows centered monopole residual with no spatial displacement (e.g. Mira pulsating giants or accretion flares).

PSF / Optical Artifact

Slight telescope jitter or FWHM differences can cause ring-like subtraction residues around saturated stars.

What Am I Seeing? Plain-English Guide

✦ Infrared Observation

Light beyond red visible wavelengths (0.75 to 5.0 µm). Cool objects like brown dwarfs and distant galaxies glow in infrared, penetrating interstellar dust clouds.

✦ Temporal Epoch

A cluster of observations taken at a distinct date or timestamp. By comparing epochs separated by weeks or years, detectives catch proper motion and brightness changes.

✦ Difference Image Subtraction

Subtracting a baseline epoch from a later epoch. Stationary stars cancel out to neutral grey, while moving bodies leave a paired dark/bright dipole.

✦ Flux (MJy/sr)

Megajanskys per steradian: the standard astronomical unit of surface brightness measured by SPHEREx detectors.

✦ Significance (>5.0σ MAD)

Signal-to-Noise Ratio calculated using Median Absolute Deviation. Detections exceeding 5σ have less than 1 in 3.5 million odds of being random Gaussian noise.

✦ Spectral Channels & Wavelength (µm)

SPHEREx splits light into 102 narrow color slices. Each slice reveals unique chemical absorption features, from water vapor to methane ice.
